= QCalc user manual
@sub A multi-function calculator for Android: type an expression the way you would write it

# intro | Introduction

## what-qcalc-does | What QCalc does
QCalc is a calculator where you type an expression as you would write it on paper and get the answer at once. The calculations are done by libqalculate, the engine of the desktop calculator Qalculate!.
- From arithmetic to fractions, roots, units, currencies, derivatives, integrals, equations and matrices, all in one line.
- When it can, the answer is exact (√2, π/2, 7/12), with a decimal approximation under it.
- Everyday calculations such as prices, loans, dates, conversions, statistics and tanks are in "Tools": type the numbers and the answer appears.
- 2D and 3D graphs, with areas, lengths and the volumes of solids of revolution.
- QCalc collects no personal data. It uses the internet only to download exchange rates.
:note: The results are for reference. Do not use them where a mistake is not allowed, such as trade or certification, design and construction, or tax returns ([notes of caution](#caution)).

## first-steps | Your first three calculations
:what: Type an expression with the keys and press [[!=]]: the answer goes into the history. While you type, the answer already shows under the input field.
:step: Open the app and type `2 + 3 × 4` with the keys. "= 14" shows under the input field.
:step: Press [[!=]]. The expression and "= 14" go into the history, and the input field is empty again.
:step: Next type `60 mph to km/h` ("to" is on the → key). "= 96.560 64 km/h" shows.
:step: The third one is `sqrt(8)`, also on the √ key: "= 2√2" and under it "≈ 2.828 427 124 746 19", the exact value and the approximation on two lines.
:example: `1/3 + 1/4` → `= 7/12`, then `≈ 0.583 333 333 333 333`
:tip: Tap an answer in the history: it goes into the input field at the cursor ([Tap to insert a result](#tap-insert)).
:mistake: The letter x is a variable, not "times". Multiply with the × key (or *).

## new-1-16 | New in 1.16.0
- **Stocks and investing**: [[Stocks and investing]] in [[Tools]] has the profit and loss of a stock with its tax (tax-free too), the average cost, the maintenance ratio and margin-call price of a margin account, the position size from a stop, yield, PER, PBR and compound growth, margin interest and lending fees by the day, and foreign stocks in your money. Only what you type is used ([Stocks and investing](#invest)).
- **Line numbers, #3 and tables in the notes**: the lines have numbers, and #3 uses the result of line 3; the numbers follow when lines are inserted, deleted or moved. In the table mode (QCalc Pro): formula columns (Amount = Qty × Price), the total and the average of each column, CSV files read and saved ([Line numbers and #3](#notes-refs), [Table mode](#notes-table)).
- **Fixed**: in Shapes and Tanks the weight is the last row of the results (without a unit, a tap chooses one). In the 3D graph, the sphere and the doughnut are one solid colored by height from the first drawing.
- **Your variables and functions**: the variables and functions you set with `k := 2` and the like, with their values, on one screen. Open it with the button [[Variables]] above the input field; a tap on a name puts it into the input field, and ⋯ changes the value or the name or deletes it (each can be undone) ([Your variables and functions](#variables)).
- **Probability distributions**: [[Distributions]] in [[Statistics]] gives the probability of a range, the density and the quantiles of the normal, binomial, Poisson, t and χ² distributions, with the range shaded in a graph ([Probability distributions](#distributions)).
- **Histogram and box plot**: for one column of data, a histogram (with a bin width you can change) and a box plot (outliers as points) ([Statistics](#stats)).
- **Regression curves and CSV**: for two columns, choose a curve of degree 2 to 4, an exponential, a logarithmic or a power curve (other than the line: QCalc Pro). [[Read a CSV file]] reads a column of a CSV file ([Statistics](#stats)).
- **Hints for the arguments of functions**: after a function name and "(", its arguments (the current one in bold), what it does and an example show under the input field. For about 60 functions; ✕ closes it ([Hints for the arguments](#arg-hints)).
- **Tap to insert a result**: a tap on an answer in the history puts it at the cursor, as a click on a cell does in a spreadsheet. Another tap before you type anything replaces it, and right after a number a visible × goes in first. The lines used get colored marks. Answers with √ or π go in in their exact form, not as rounded decimals ([Tap to insert a result](#tap-insert)).
- **Line numbers and #12 in the history**: the lines of the history have numbers, and `#12` in an expression is the answer of line 12; editing line 12 can work out again the lines that use #12. [[Select: sum and average]] in ⋯ starts with that line and shows the sum at once, and touching and holding a line chooses a range. [[Copy as a table]] copies the expressions and answers as two columns for a spreadsheet ([Line numbers and #12](#hist-refs), [Adding up lines](#select-sum)). A tap on the number of a line puts #12 in, and Copy as a table can copy the answers only (1 column).
- **The forms of answers**: an expression of decimals only keeps a decimal answer (123456.789, 2.5, 0.5; the fraction with [[As a fraction]]), and an answer has two forms at most (10/4 = 5/2 = 2.5) ([Reading the answer](#results)).
- **Editing a line**: the line and its answer stay in sight, and the lines worked out again flash for a moment. A line keeps the angle unit it was worked out with and is edited with it. AC stops editing too, and [[Cancel]] in the question about the lines below goes back to editing ([Editing a line of the history](#edit-line)).
- **Other fixes**: while "Volume and surface" is open in the 3D graph, the legend shows the color of the solid being measured. Money rounds a half cent up correctly (some sums were one cent short). Results are copied without digit grouping. `nPr(5, 2)` and `nCr(5, 2)` work. More messages of the engine (the note on 6/2(1+2) and others) are in your language. The converter leaves out rarely used units (they can still be typed).

## new-1-15 | What is new in 1.15.0
- **Easier results**: results have 15 significant digits, in groups of three. Phones that still had the earlier defaults (10 digits, no groups) change once with this version. [[Precision (significant digits)]] and [[Digit grouping (1,000)]] in the [[Settings]] set them back ([Reading the answer](#results)).
- **Reading into the calculation notes**: [[More ▾]] → [[Open a file]] in the notes reads a .txt or .csv file, and text or a file sent with “Share” → QCalc from another app is taken in too ([Calculation notes](#notes)).
- **Taylor series**: taylor on the [[Calculus]] page gives the Taylor polynomial about a center up to a degree (exact coefficients; `taylor(sin(x), x, 0, 5)` can be typed too). [[Compare in a graph]] draws it with the function ([Derivatives, integrals, limits, sums](#calculus)).
- **Three new kinds of 2D graph**: parametric (`x = cos(t), y = sin(2t)`), polar (`r = 1 + cos(θ)`) and implicit (`x^2 + y^2 = 1`) lines, told from their form or chosen at the top; their length and area (polar), and new samples: a cardioid, a rose, a Lissajous curve and more ([2D graph](#graph-2d)).
- **Sliders**: letters such as a and b in the formulas of a graph get sliders, and moving one changes the graph at once; ▶ keeps it moving. Free in 2D; the sliders in 3D and the GIF of a slider are QCalc Pro ([2D graph](#graph-2d)).
- **Next steps**: buttons under the newest answer for details, a graph, factoring, the derivative and more; a button's calculation becomes a new line ([Reading answers](#results)).
- **Details**: the exact value, decimal, fraction, bases and prime factors of an answer, or the graph, zeros, derivative, integral and Taylor series of an expression, on one sheet ([Reading answers](#results)).
- **Two lines**: an exact result with its approximation shows "= exact" on one line and "≈ approximation" on the next. Fractions and roots are drawn in the same letters as the other results.
- **Numbers**: no ending zeros in approximations; very large or small numbers as 1.606 938 × 10⁶⁰ (copied as 1.606938E60); whole numbers and decimals that end are shown in full up to 60 digits, even beyond the precision (for example 30!).
- **Long results and units**: a result too long for one line gets smaller letters, and only then breaks between groups of three digits; a result with units stays on one line (m/s).
- **The history**: its expressions are written with ×, √, π and raised powers (2^10 → 2¹⁰).
- **Editing a line of the history**: tap the expression of a line to edit it in place; = puts the new answer there, and the lines below can be worked out again ([Editing a line of the history](#edit-line)).
- **The precision at hand**: the row at the top shows the precision (e.g. 15 dig); press it to choose 10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 100 or Other…, and the expression being typed is worked out again at once. ⋯ → [[Work it out with other digits]] in the history shows one line with other digits ([Reading the answer](#results)).
- **3D close to real things**: 3D is drawn by the device's GPU (WebGL 2), with real light and shadows: the 3D graph, [[!⟳ 3D]] in the 2D graph, the solids and tanks of the shapes, and the solid from a profile ([3D graph](#graph-3d)).
- **See-through**: [[See-through]] lets you see through surfaces and solids: their back and inner faces and where two surfaces meet. The slider sets how opaque, and moving it while the see-through is off turns it on; with two things or more, choose which are see-through (for example only a tank's [[Container]]).
- **Turn it any way**: one finger turns 3D in any direction, over the top and under the bottom too. The cube button gives [[From the front]], [[From above]] or [[From the side]] at once.
- **Materials**: a solid looks like the material chosen in [[Material (density)]], and a tank's liquid like water, seawater, kerosene or gasoline. New materials: stainless steel, aluminium, copper, brass, wood, PLA, ABS and acrylic.
- **3D once for free**: without QCalc Pro, 3D can be opened once for free ([What is free](#pro-free)).
- **Rate on Google Play**: [[Rate on Google Play]] in [[About this app]] opens QCalc's page in Google Play for your stars and review.
- **The range of a graph**: a and b typed right after drawing a graph are no longer replaced by the range the app finds ([Area and length](#area-length)).

# basics | Basic calculations

## typing | Typing an expression
:what: Type an expression as a math book writes it, with the keys or, after ⌨, with the phone's keyboard.
:step: Type the expression with the digit and operator keys. Parentheses are on the ( ) keys.
:step: A function key of the Functions page (sin, log, √ and others) types "sin(" and the like.
:step: Write units and currencies by name (`5 km`, `100 USD`). Convert with "to" (`5 km to mi`).
:step: Press [[!=]] to calculate.
:example: `2^10` → `= 1024`, `5!` → `= 120`, `sin(30°)` → `= 1/2`
:tip: ÷ × + and the like in an empty input field continue from the last answer, "ans".
:tip: A column or a table copied in a spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets) — two or more values on lines or with tabs — pasted into the input field asks what to do: add them up with +, put them into the calculation notes as lines, use them as data of the statistics, or make a matrix (two or more columns). Cells of words only (headings) are left out. One line pastes as it is.
:tip: [[Make them lines of the history (worked out one by one)]] takes one expression from each row and works them out from the top as if each were typed and [[!=]] pressed, adding them to the history (from QCalc's [[Copy as a table]], the column of the expressions). Rows that cannot be worked out are left out and named; [[⤶ Undo]] takes all the lines put in away.
:mistake: Parentheses that do not pair up give an error. The message says what to fix.

## results | Reading the answer: exact, approximate, fractions
:what: An answer that starts with "=" is exact; one that starts with "≈" is approximate. Answers with fractions or √ also show the decimal, on the next line.
- `1/3 + 1/4` → `= 7/12`, then `≈ 0.583 333 333 333 333` (the fraction stays, with its decimal)
- `sqrt(8)` → `= 2√2`, then `≈ 2.828 427 124 746 19`
- `30!` → `= 265,252,859,812,191,058,636,308,480,000,000` (whole numbers in full up to 60 digits)
- `2^200` → `≈ 1.606 938 044 258 99 × 10⁶⁰` (a very large number)
- `2.5`, `0.1 + 0.2` → `= 2.5`, `= 0.3` (an expression of decimals only has a decimal answer; [[As a fraction]] shows the fraction)
- `10/4` → `= 5/2 = 2.5` (two forms at most)
:step: The chip at the top switches between [[!EXACT]], approximate and automatic.
:step: The precision (15 digits by default) can be changed with the chip “15 dig” in the row at the top. It offers 10, 15, 20, 30, 50, 100 and Other… (type a number); the answer of the expression being typed is worked out again at once, and the answers in the history stay as they are. [[Precision (significant digits)]] in [[Settings]] changes it too. Results are shown with their digits in groups of three ([[Digit grouping (1,000)]] turns it off).
:step: To see one line of the history with more digits, choose 30, 50, 100 or Other… in its ⋯ → [[Work it out with other digits]]. The answer shows in a sheet with [[Put the expression into the input field]], [[Keep it in the history]] and [[Copy the result]]. The precision of the settings and the line stay as they are.
:pro: Up to 1000 digits are free. More than 1000, up to 200000 digits, is a QCalc Pro feature.
:tip: A very long answer scrolls in the history so that every digit can be read; copying it copies every digit, without digit grouping.
:step: Under the newest answer, buttons for what is often done next (6 at most): for an expression in x [[Details]] · [[Graph]] · [[Factor]] · [[Derivative]] · [[Integral]] · [[Solve = 0]]; for a number [[Details]], [[As a fraction]], [[Scientific]] (an integer: [[Prime factors]], [[In hex]]); with a unit [[Convert…]]; for a matrix [[Determinant]], [[Inverse]], [[Eigenvalues]], [[Transpose]]. A button's calculation becomes a new line of the history.
:step: [[Details]] (or ⋯ → [[Details]]) shows the other forms of the answer together: for a number the exact value, the decimal, the fraction, the mixed fraction and the scientific notation (an integer in bases 2, 8 and 16 and its prime factors; with a unit, other units); for an expression in x a small graph, the zeros, the derivative, the integral, the Taylor series and the factored form. Tap a row for [[Put the expression in the field]], [[Keep it in the history]] or [[Copy result]].
:tip: Without these buttons: [[Settings]] → [[Suggest the next steps under the newest answer]] off.

## undo | Deleting and undoing
:what: ⌫ deletes one character, AC empties the input field. Right after that, [[Undo]] above the input field brings it back.
:step: ⌫ deletes the character before the cursor; hold it to keep deleting.
:step: AC empties the input field, and [[Undo]] shows above it.
:step: [[Undo]] brings the expression back.
:tip: A line deleted from the history can also be brought back for a few seconds with [[Undo]] at the bottom of the screen.

## number-format | Number format: the decimal sign
:what: Choose whether the decimal sign is "." or "," and how digits are grouped. At first QCalc writes numbers as your phone's region does.
:step: Open [[Settings]].
:step: In [[Number format]], choose [[Automatic]], 1,234.5, 1.234,5 or 1 234,5.
:step: What you type and what you see follow the format you chose.
:example: With 1.234,5, type two and a half as `2,5`: `2,5 + 3,75` → `= 6,25`
:tip: With a decimal comma, separate the arguments of a function with ";": `max(1,5; 2)`.
:mistake: Typing `1.5` with a decimal comma gives an error; the message shows the right way to write it.

# history | History and memory
:what: The expressions and answers stay in the history, up to 300 lines. 🕘 at the top right searches, adds up chosen lines, exports and clears.
:step: A line works in two ways, by where you tap it: **tap its answer** to use the value ([[!=]] makes a new line; [Tap to insert a result](#tap-insert)); **tap its expression** to edit the line ([[!=]] replaces it; [Editing a line of the history](#edit-line)).
:step: ⋯ at the left of a line offers [[☆ Pin at the top]] and [[Add a short note]].
:step: 🕘 offers [[Search the history]], [[Select lines: sum, average, copy]], [[Export the history (CSV file)]] and [[Clear the whole history]].
:shot: history | The history menu of 🕘
:tip: Lines pinned with a star stay when the history is full.

## tap-insert | Tap to insert a result
:what: A tap on an answer in the history puts it into the input field at the cursor, as a click on a cell does while you type a formula in a spreadsheet.
:step: When the field is empty, or right after a calculation, the answer simply goes in.
:step: An answer tapped into the empty field shows a pale band above the field, “Making a new line from the answer of #51”: [[!=]] makes a new line. To change that line instead, press [[Edit this line]] in the band (also after changing numbers in the field: the line is then edited with the field as it is). The band also goes with ✕, AC or an empty field.
:step: Another tap on an answer before you type anything replaces the one tapped before.
:step: After an operator such as + or ×, the answer goes in after it. Right after a number, a letter or ")", a visible × goes in first.
:step: The lines used in the expression get colored marks; deleting the answer from the field removes its mark.
:example: Tap the line `120`, press ×, tap the line `3` → the field holds `120 × 3`, and the answer is `= 360`
:tip: Answers with √ or π go in in their exact form, not as rounded decimals: the answer of `sqrt(8)` goes in as `(2 × √(2))`, so its square is `= 8`. An answer without an exact form goes in with all the digits of the precision.
:tip: To use a line by its number, choose "Insert #12" in its ⋯; to copy an answer, [[Copy result]] in ⋯ (copied without digit grouping).
:tip: A tap on the number at the left of a line (#12) puts #12 into the expression instead of the value, as pointing at a cell in a spreadsheet ([Line numbers and #12](#hist-refs)).

## edit-line | Editing a line of the history
:what: Tap the expression of a line (the small one above its answer), or [[Edit expression]] in its ⋯, to edit that line. The expression goes into the input field, with the band "Editing #12" above it (12: the number of the line). The history scrolls so that the line and its answer stay in sight.
:step: Change the expression and press [[!=]]. The line gets the new expression and answer in the same place. ★ and the short note stay, and the menu ⋯ shows the time of the change beside the time of the line.
:step: While you type, the line shows the expression and its new answer in its place, with the line as it was in small grey letters below it (“Before: …”). A line can be edited in the line itself or in the field below: tap the expression in the line to put the caret there, and the keys, ◀ ▶, ⌫, ⌨, pasting and taps on other answers work there; tap the field to go on typing there. What is typed in one shows at once in the other. The history itself changes only when you press [[!=]].
:step: "[[Edited]]" and [[Undo]] show for a few seconds. [[Undo]] brings back the expression and answer as they were.
:step: When the lines below use the answer of this line (ans or #12) or a variable it defines, the app asks whether to work them out again ("Also the 2 lines below") or [[Only this line]]. The lines worked out again flash for a moment, and [[Undo]] takes them back too. With [[Only this line]], the lines below get a note that their answer may be out of date. [[Cancel]] changes nothing: the new expression stays in the field and you go on editing.
:step: To work it out as a new line below instead, press [[As a new line]] in the band, then [[!=]]. ✕ in the band, AC or the back button stops editing and changes nothing.
:tip: A line keeps the angle unit (degrees, radians) it was worked out with. Editing it, working out the lines below again and other digits use that unit, and the band says so. Lines worked out before 1.16 use the unit set now; with a function of angles the band says so.
:tip: The other settings (base and the like) are those of now. A line worked out in another base says so in the band and gets the base of now.
:tip: Deleting the line being edited, or clearing the history, ends the editing; the expression in the field is then worked out as a new line.
:tip: A tap on the answer of a line puts it into the input field ([Tap to insert a result](#tap-insert)).

## hist-refs | Line numbers and #12
:what: Every line of the history has a number (the small "#12" under its ⋯). `#12` in an expression is the answer of line 12, as a reference to a cell in a spreadsheet.
:step: Choose "Insert #12 (this line by its number)" in the ⋯ of a line: `#12` goes into the input field. Typing `#` and the number works too.
:step: The answer with `#12` shows under the input field while you type.
:step: A number belongs to its line: deleting other lines does not change it, and no number is given twice.
:step: When line 12 is edited, the app asks whether to work out again the lines below that use `#12` ([Editing a line of the history](#edit-line)).
:example: Line 12 is `= 250` and line 13 is `= 3`: `#12 × #13` → `= 750`
:mistake: The number of a deleted line, or of a line below the one being edited, cannot be used; a message says so. When a line used is deleted, a note under the line says so.
:tip: It is written as #3 is in the notes ([Line numbers and #3](#notes-refs)).

## pins-notes | Pins, notes and search
:step: Choose [[☆ Pin at the top]] in the ⋯ of a line: it moves up with the other pinned lines.
:step: Choose [[Add a short note]] in ⋯ and write a short note. It shows above the expression.
:step: In 🕘, [[Search the history]] shows only the lines whose expression, answer or note has the words you type.
:example: A line noted "rent" is found by searching for "rent".

## select-sum | Adding up lines
:step: Choose [[Select lines: sum, average, copy]] in 🕘. [[Select: sum and average]] in the ⋯ of a line starts with that line chosen and shows the sum at once.
:step: Tap the lines to add up. Touching and holding a line chooses all the lines from the one chosen last to it.
:step: The sum and the average show below, with their unit when the units agree. Lines shown as decimals add up to a decimal. [[Copy]] copies the lines (without digit grouping).
:step: [[Copy as a table]] copies the expressions and answers of the chosen lines in two columns, with a tab between them: pasted into a spreadsheet, they make a column of expressions and a column of answers.
:step: [[Copy as a table]] lets you choose "Expression and answer (2 columns)" or "Answers only (1 column)"; the one chosen last comes first.
:tip: [[Copy as a table]] is free. Saving the whole history as a CSV file ([Exporting the history](#export)) is a QCalc Pro feature.
:mistake: Lines in different units (m and kg) cannot be added; the reason is shown.

## export | Exporting the history (CSV)
:pro:
:what: Saves the history as a CSV file that spreadsheets can open.
:step: Choose [[Export the history (CSV file)]] in 🕘.
:step: Choose where to save it on Android's save screen.
:tip: To paste only the chosen lines into a spreadsheet, use [[Copy as a table]] ([Adding up lines](#select-sum)).

## memory-keys | Memory keys (M+ M− MR MC)
:what: The memory keys of a pocket calculator. The value is kept in its exact form.
:step: Open [[More settings]] in [[Settings]] and turn on [[Show the memory keys (M+ M− MR MC)]].
:step: With an answer shown, M+ adds it to the memory and M− subtracts it.
:step: MR puts the memory into the input field; MC clears it.

# keys | Keys and typing help

## key-pages | The four pages of keys
:what: The keys are on four pages, switched with the tabs above them.
| Page | Main keys |

## writing | Writing functions, units and currencies
:what: Expressions are written as in Qalculate!. The common ways:
| Write | Meaning |
| `sqrt(x)`, `√x` | Square root |
| `x^2` | Power |
| `5 km to mi` | Convert a unit |
| `100 USD to EUR` | Convert a currency (with the exchange rates) |
| `solve(x^2 − 5x + 6 = 0)` | Solve an equation |
| `diff(x^3)` | Derivative |
| `integrate(x^2, 0, 1)` | Definite integral |
| `integrate(x^2, x)`, `sum(k, k, 1, 10)` | Indefinite integral, sum. With the variable second, it is read in the order integrate(expression, lower limit, upper limit, variable) |

| `9!!`, `9!!!` | double factorial (9 × 7 × 5 × 3 × 1), multifactorial (9 × 6 × 3); "!!" is not a factorial taken twice |

## currencies | Currencies and exchange rates
:what: Currencies convert like `100 USD to EUR`. The exchange rates are updated by themselves when the app is opened (when it starts or when you come back to it; an internet connection is needed). While roaming abroad or in Data Saver they are not updated by themselves; press [[Update now]] when you need new rates.
:step: To update them now, press [[Update exchange rates now]] in [[Settings]].
:step: The time of the last update is on the same row.
:mistake: Without the internet the rates cannot be updated; the last ones are used.

The prices of Bitcoin (BTC) and of gold (XAU), silver (XAG), platinum (XPT) and palladium (XPD) are updated with the exchange rates, too.

The rates are kept on the device, so where there is no internet you can still convert with the last rates. Opening the app once before a trip is a good idea.

The physical constants are the CODATA 2022 values built into the calculation engine; they are not taken from the internet. New recommended values come with updates of the app.

### pnl | Stock profit and loss
:what: From the prices bought and sold, the shares and the fees of buying and selling: the profit or loss, in %, the tax, after tax, what the sale brings in, and the break-even price.
:step: Open [[Stock profit and loss]] and type the prices and the shares.
:step: A fee can be an amount (500) or a share of the trade (0.1%).
:step: Choose [[Taxed]] or [[Tax-free account]]. The tax rate is 20.315 % (Japan's) unless you type another.
:example: Bought at 1000, sold at 1200, 100 shares, no fees → +20,000.00, tax 4,063.00, after tax +15,937.00
:tip: The % is of what was paid, with the fee. The break-even price is where selling leaves no loss after the fees.
:note: The tax is worked out on this trade alone. At 20.315 %, the income taxes (15.315 %) and the resident tax (5 %) are each rounded down and added, as a broker withholds them (a profit of 19 yen pays 2). Offsetting losses in the year is not taken into account.

### avg-cost | Average cost
:what: The average cost of shares bought in several buys, and how many shares at what price bring the average to a target.
:step: Open [[Average cost]] and choose [[The average of the buys]] or [[How to reach an average]].
:step: The average of the buys: one buy to a line, "price shares" (for example 1000 100).
:step: To reach an average: type the shares held, their average and the target, then the price or the shares to buy.
:example: 100 at 1000 and 200 at 900 → average 933.33 (300 shares, 280,000.00)
:example: 100 at an average of 1200 → buying at 900, 200 shares bring the average to 1000
- Average = (price × shares + …) / all the shares
- Shares to reach a target T = N × (A − T) / (T − P) (N: shares held, A: their average, P: the price; rounded up to the trading unit)
:tip: The trading unit is 100 shares unless you type another; for stocks traded one by one, type 1.

### margin | Margin: maintenance ratio
:what: From the margin and the position: the maintenance ratio, and the price at which it falls to the margin call.
:step: Open [[Margin: maintenance ratio]] and type the cash and, if any, the stocks as margin (after the haircut).
:step: Choose [[Bought]] or [[Sold short]], and type the entry price, the shares and the price now. Costs (interest, fees) can be typed too.
:step: The line of the margin call is 20 % unless you type another.
:example: 1,000,000 cash, 3,000 shares bought at 1000, now 900 → 23.33 %, margin call at 866.67 (−3.70 % from now)
The formulas:
- Ratio = (cash + stocks as margin + profit or loss − costs) / value of the positions × 100
- Value of the positions = entry price × shares (under [[More ▾]], other positions can be added with their profit or loss)
- Profit or loss: bought (now − entry) × shares, sold short (entry − now) × shares. A gain is not counted unless chosen under [[More ▾]], [[Unrealized gains]]
- Margin-call price: bought, entry − (cash + stocks as margin − costs − line × value) / shares; sold short, entry + (the same) / shares
:note: Brokers count gains, haircuts and costs differently, and check at different times (usually the close).
:pro:

### position-size | Position size from a stop
:what: From the capital, the loss you allow in one trade (% of the capital), the entry and the stop: the shares (rounded down to the trading unit) and the loss at the stop. With a take-profit price, the risk:reward ratio.
:step: Open [[Position size from a stop]] and type the capital, the risk, the entry and the stop.
:step: A stop below the entry is a buy; above it, a short.
:example: 3,000,000 capital, 1 %, buy at 1000, stop at 950 (unit 100) → 600 shares, loss at the stop 30,000.00; take profit at 1150: risk:reward 1 : 3.00
- Shares = capital × risk % / |entry − stop| (rounded down to the trading unit)
- Risk:reward = 1 : |take profit − entry| / |entry − stop|
:pro:

### distributions | Probability distributions
:what: For the normal, binomial, Poisson, t and χ² distributions: the probability of a range a ≤ X ≤ b, the density (P(X = a) for the binomial and Poisson) and the quantile, with the range shaded in a graph.

:step: Type its parameters (the mean μ and the standard deviation σ for the normal, the trials n and the probability of success p for the binomial).
:step: Type a and b of the range. Leave one empty for no limit on that side (P(X ≤ b) or P(a ≤ X)).
:step: Type a cumulative probability after [[Quantile: P(X ≤ x) =]] for its x (for the binomial and Poisson the smallest k with P(X ≤ k) at least that), dashed in the graph.

:tip: The same functions work in the input field: `normdist(1.96, 0, 1, 1)` (P(X ≤ 1.96)), `normdistinv(0.975, 0, 1)`, `binomdist(3, 10, 0.5)` (P(X = 3)), `poisson(2, 3, 1)`, `tdist(2, 10, 1)`, `tdistinv(0.975, 10)`, `chisqdist(7.81, 3, 1)`, `chisqdistinv(0.95, 3)`. :tip: A line that begins with # (but not # and a digit) or with // is a note (it is not calculated). #3, with a digit just after #, is the result of line 3.
:step: To read a file, press [[Open a file]] under [[More ▾]] and choose a .txt or .csv file. A .txt gives one line for each line; a .csv saved by QCalc gives its column “Calculation”, another .csv its first column. UTF-8 and Shift_JIS (a Japanese CSV made by Excel) can be read.
:step: In another app (a notes app, mail …), choose some text or a .txt or .csv file, then QCalc in that app’s “Share”: the calculation notes open.
:step: Then choose [[As a new memo]] or [[At the end of this memo]]; for a single line, [[Put it into the input field]] too. A new memo is named after the file (without its extension).
:tip: What is read is only text, worked out by the same rules as typed lines. A file over 1 MB, or more than 300 lines (what a memo holds), is not read, and the app says so.

### notes-refs | Line numbers and #3
:what: The lines of the notes have their numbers. #3 uses the result of line 3 (a line above). When a line above changes, the lines that use it are worked out again at once.
:step: Tap the number of a line for [[Insert a line above]], [[Insert a line below]], [[Move up]], [[Move down]] and [[Delete this line]].
:step: While another line is being written, tap a line number to write #3 (or its own number) in it.
:example: lines 1 to 3 rent, electricity and water, line 4 "total", line 5 `#4 / 2` → ¥47,150 (94,300 / 2)
:tip: When lines are inserted, deleted or moved, the numbers written in the lines follow, so they still mean the same lines. A reference to a deleted line becomes #?, and the line says so.
:note: #3 can only be a line above the line it is in.
:tip: A line moved above a line it uses (its #4) cannot be worked out: the app says so ("Line 1 uses #2, so it cannot be worked out above #2") with [[Undo]].

### notes-table | Table mode
:what: A memo as a table: columns with names, a column worked out from the others by a formula (such as Amount = Qty × Price), and the total and the average of each column. A cell can have a unit or a currency, such as 3 kg or 120 JPY.
:step: Press [[Table]] above the memo. It starts with an example: Item, Qty, Price and Amount (Qty × Price).
:step: Type a value in a cell: the formula columns, the totals and the averages are worked out again at once.
:step: Tap the name of a column for [[Rename]], [[Work it out by a formula]] (for example Qty × Price), and to insert, move or delete a column; tap a row number to insert, move or delete a row.
:step: [[+ Row]] and [[+ Column]] add a row and a column. A column added as "Amount = Qty × Price" is a formula column.
:example: 3 apples at 120 JPY, 2 bread at 250 JPY, 1 milk at 180 JPY → amounts ¥360, ¥500, ¥180, total ¥1040, average ≈ ¥346.67
:tip: A cell with words only (the name of an item) is left out of the totals. Values in different units (3 kg and 500 g) of the same kind are added up together.
:tip: A cell with a digit that cannot be read as a value ("apples 2 pcs") is also left out of the total and the average.
:tip: A table copied in a spreadsheet and pasted into one cell goes into the cells from there to the right and down; the rows and columns missing are added (up to 12 columns). Pasted into the top left cell with a first row of headings (no digits), the table becomes the one pasted, its first row the names of the columns. A formula column keeps its formula.
:tip: Values of different kinds in a column (3 kg and 2) make the total and the average say "Values of different units are mixed". The average of a column of money has 2 decimals (≈ ¥346.67).
:step: [[Save as CSV]] under [[More ▾]] saves the table as a CSV file (the row numbers, the names of the columns, the values, the total and the average). A CSV file chosen with [[Open a file]] can be read [[As a table (the first row: the names of the columns)]].
:tip: On a narrow screen, swipe the table sideways for the other columns; the column of the row numbers stays on the left.
:pro: The table mode (with saving as CSV and reading a CSV file as a table) is a QCalc Pro feature. The line numbers and #3 are free.

# graphs | Graphs

## graph-2d | 2D graph
:what: Draws graphs of y = f(x). Drag to move them, pinch to zoom.
:step: Open [[Graph]] from ☰ (or 📈 above the input field).
:step: Type the functions, one per line, and the range of x.
:step: Press [[Draw]].
:step: Tap the graph to see the coordinates of a point.
:step: The kind of each line is told from its form; [[!y = f(x)]], [[Parametric]], [[Polar]] and [[Implicit]] at the top choose it too (another kind than the box has puts an example of it in the box).
:example: parametric `x = cos(t), y = sin(2t)` (with a range of t), polar `r = 1 + cos(θ)` (a range of θ, 0 to 2π at first; t works in place of θ), implicit `x^2 + y^2 = 1`, `x^3 + y^3 = 3x y` (an equation with both x and y)
:tip: Parametric and polar lines take angles in radians. An implicit line joins the points of a grid where left side − right side changes its sign, with a finer grid where the line is intricate; moving or zooming draws it again for the new view.
:tip: A parametric or polar line has buttons for its [[Length]] and [[Area]] (polar, or a parametric line that closes). The polar area is ½∫r² dθ over the range of θ (what is gone round twice counts twice); a parametric line that crosses itself has no area. [[Calculus ▾]], [[Area and length ▾]] and [[Make a solid ▾]] are for lines y = f(x).
:tip: Letters such as a and b in the formulas get sliders under the graph (6 at most). Moving one draws the graph again at once. Tap the value to type a number; ▶ moves it to and fro. ⋯ changes its range (−5 to 5 at first) and step (0.1).
:tip: The letters with sliders are a to z and A to Z, one letter each, but e, i, t, x, y and E, I, X, Y. Their values are used in this graph only: in the calculator a stays the are, c the speed of light and g the gram. A variable with a value of your own (as k := 2) keeps that value, and a letter used as a function, as f in f(x), gets no slider. Write a letter apart from x: `a x` or `a*x` (`ax` is one name).
:tip: [[Calculus ▾]] gives the derivative, tangents, zeros and extrema.
:tip: Tap a sample below the graph to draw an example.

## solid | Make a solid (solids of revolution)
:what: The volume and surface of the solid made by turning a region of the 2D graph around an axis.
:svg: solid | The region between y = x and y = x² turned around the x axis
:step: Draw the functions in the 2D graph (for example `x` and `x^2`).
:step: Press the round button [[!⟳ 3D]] on the graph. The solid around the x axis appears in 3D at once, with its volume large above it (QCalc Pro). Without Pro, the 2D graph shows the solid's cut (mirrored across the axis) and its volume.
:step: For another axis, go back with [[← Back to 2D]] and tap its line; drag the line y = k with a finger. [[View in 3D]] shows it in 3D again.
:step: [[↶ Undo]] goes back to the axis before.
:shot: solid | The region between y = x and y = x² turned around the x axis
:example: Between y = x and y = x², around the x axis → volume 2π/15 ≈ 0.418 879
:tip: [[Show the method with lines]] draws the disks (washers) or shells. The line [[Pappus–Guldinus theorem: ]] compares the volume from the centroid with the integral.
:pro: Turning the solid with a finger in 3D is a QCalc Pro feature, like the 3D graph. Without Pro, the row [[Turn it in 3D (QCalc Pro)]] appears instead.

# forms | Forms: calculus, equations, matrices

## calculus | Derivatives, integrals, limits, sums
:what: The keys of the [[Calculus]] page open a form with boxes □.
:step: Press d/dx, ∫, lim, Σ or another key on the [[Calculus]] page.
:step: Fill in the boxes. The variable can stay x.
:step: The answer shows below; [[!=]] keeps it in the history.
:shot: form | The form of an integral
:example: ∫ x² sin(x) dx from 0 to π → `= π² − 4`, then `≈ 5.869 604 401 089 36`
:tip: A limit can be [[Both sides]], [[From the right (+)]] or [[From the left (−)]].
:step: taylor ([[Taylor series]]) on the [[Calculus]] page takes a function f(x), a center a (0 at first) and a degree n (5 at first, up to 20) and gives the Taylor polynomial from its lowest degree up, as `x − x³/6 + x⁵/120 + O(x⁶)`. The coefficients stay exact where they can (fractions, √, π). `taylor(sin(x), x, 0, 5)` typed in the input field works too.
:step: [[Compare in a graph]] draws the function and its Taylor polynomial together in the 2D graph.
:tip: When the function or a derivative has no finite value at the center (√x or 1/x at x = 0), the app says so; a value given by a limit, as sin(x)/x at x = 0, works. When it takes long, try a lower degree.

## equations | Equations

:step: The answer is shown as "x = 2 or x = 3".
:example: `solve(x^2 − 5x + 6 = 0)` → x = 2, x = 3

## digits | Digits shown by each tool
:what: Each tool can show its results with its own number of digits. Open [[Digits shown…]] from ⋯ at the right of the tool's title. Shapes (with Tanks), Solid from a profile and the graph (area, length, Make a solid, 3D) share one setting.
:step: In a tool, press ⋯ and choose [[Digits shown…]].
:step: Choose [[Recommended (automatic)]], [[Significant digits]] or [[Decimals]], and set the number with + and −. Each tool keeps its own choice.
:step: With digits chosen, [[Trailing zeros]] are [[Keep (2.50)]] (at first: exactly as many digits as chosen, 1.000000000) or [[Drop (2.5)]] (1).
:step: A tool that does not use its default digits has the mark "[[changed]]" in the settings' list and in [[Digits shown…]] of its ⋯.
:example: The volume of a cylinder 11,250π cm³ → recommended 11,250π ≈ 35,342.9 cm³; with 2 decimal places 11,250π ≈ 35,342.92 cm³; with 4 significant digits 11,250π ≈ 35,340 cm³
:tip: For Shapes, Tanks, Solid from a profile and the graph, [[More: each kind, the 3D picture, rounding, CSV ▾]] also sets [[Each kind of quantity on its own]] (volume, area, length, weight and %), the numbers [[In the 3D picture]] (recommended: 3 significant digits, % with one decimal), the rounding ([[Round half up]], [[Round down]] or [[Round up]]) and the quick-reference tables saved as CSV ([[All the digits]] or [[As shown]]). Exact values (11250π) stay; only the numbers after "≈" change.
There are three ways back. For one tool, press [[Back to the default (recommended)]] in ⋯ → [[Digits shown…]]. For every tool, press [[All back to the default (recommended)]] under [[Digits shown by each tool]] in the settings (the calculator's [[Precision (significant digits)]] goes back to 15 and [[Maximum decimal places]] to no limit as well). For everything, press [[Reset all settings]] in the settings (the settings and the digits of each tool go back to their defaults; the language stays).
The recommended digits are a digit or two more than are usually needed (no digits are added to values that are exact). Money is shown to the smallest unit of the currency of your region (1 yen, 0.01 dollar), with its zeros (19.90); Shapes, Tanks and Solid from a profile have 6 significant digits after "≈", the area, the length, Make a solid and the 3D values of the graph 8 (at least 5 decimals), and % one decimal. Recommended digits have no zeros at the end, but for money, unit prices and statistics.

The calculation is made with 12 digits more than are shown (at least 4 near the limit of the precision; numeric integration 4 more, 24 digits at most); the result is rounded for showing.

Numeric integration (areas, lengths and volumes of the graph) is certain to about 20 digits: more than 20 are not shown. The numbers worked out with double precision (the tools that calculate in JavaScript: price, percentages, unit price, statistics and others; the height of a liquid, the tables of the tanks, Solid from a profile, the 3D picture) show 12 digits at most.
:tip: The calculator, the forms and the notes use [[Precision (significant digits)]] in the [[Settings]].

# trouble | Troubleshooting

## faq | Frequently asked questions
- **The answer is not a decimal**: check the chip at the top or [[Exact or approximate]].
- **x gives strange answers**: x is a variable; multiply with the × key.
- **The currency rates are old**: press [[Update exchange rates now]] (with the internet).
- **The top row of keys is missing**: it is hidden with a large text size; ⌨ at the right end of the tabs shows it.
- **The 3D graph does not open**: the 3D graph is a QCalc Pro feature; without Pro it can be tried once for free ([QCalc Pro](#pro)).

## errors | Error messages and how to fix them
:what: An error message says what is wrong and how to fix it. The common ones:
| Message | How to fix it |
| Parentheses do not pair up | Make ( and ) the same number |
| 1.5 cannot be read (with a decimal comma) | Type 1,5; separate arguments with ";" |
| The units do not match | Add quantities in the same kind of unit (m and kg cannot be added) |
| Draw a function first | Press [[Draw]] in the graph before opening the area or the solid |
| The time limit was reached | Make the [time limit](#time-limit) longer, or the expression simpler |

## time-limit | The time limit of a calculation
:what: A very long calculation stops after [[Time limit (seconds)]] (20 seconds by default, 1 to 3600).
:step: "Calculating…" shows while it runs; tap it to stop.
:step: To allow longer, change [[Time limit (seconds)]] in [[More settings]] in [[Settings]].

## caution | Notes of caution: using the results
:note: The results of QCalc are for reference. Do not use them for trade or certification (including legal metrology), design and construction, tax returns, medicine or decisions about safety.
- The amount in a tank assumes the ideal shape of the tank; a real tank may differ.
- Weights use typical densities.
- The tax rates are the common rates of November 2026 and can be changed in the box.
- Exchange rates are those downloaded, not the rates of a real trade.
- The tools for money or safety show a line of caution under the results.
